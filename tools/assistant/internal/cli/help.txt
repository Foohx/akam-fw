akam - guided setup for Akam (PC side)

Usage: ./akam [options] [command]

Commands:
  (none)             home screen: choose the camera, set up, update, options, backup, settings
                     (on a terminal: a full-screen menu, keys: arrows or j/k, Enter, ? help, c camera, q)
  steps              the nine setup steps with their status, one at a time; resumes where you stopped
  doctor             check this computer (tools); changes nothing
  get-kit            download and verify the prebuilt kit (no compiling)
  build              build from source instead (optional, needs more tools)
  prepare-backup-sd  prepare the microSD card used to back up the camera
  find-camera        find the camera (asks its IP address, checks the ports)
  backup             back up the camera (double read, checked, copied to ~/akam-backup-<name>-<date>/),
                     or accept a backup folder you made by hand (--backup-dir DIR)
  image              build the Akam image from your backup
  prepare-flash-sd   prepare the microSD card used to flash the camera
  flash              write the image to the camera (SSH when Akam runs, else maintenance telnet)
  verify             check SSH, ports and hardware, then the same without the card
  update             flash a new image over SSH (no card)
  hostname [NAME]    give the camera its network name (default: the camera's name), over SSH;
                     applied at the next power cycle (needs a firmware that has akam_hostname.sh)
  rtsp-auth [set|disable|status]
                     password (Digest login) on the video stream, over SSH; off by default
                     (typed here, never shown, sent to the camera only as a hash); default: status
  led [show | status on|off|auto] [ir auto|off]
                     the front LED and the infrared night LEDs, over SSH; saved on the camera. Default: show.
                       status: on (as now), off, or auto (blinks while starting or without Wi-Fi, blinks
                       slowly while someone watches the video, solid otherwise)
                       ir: auto (IR LEDs light at night) or off (never lit; night mode still switches the image)
  audio [show | volume 0-100 | beep | on | off | talk on|off | mic on|off]
                     the camera's speaker and microphone, over SSH. Default: show (volume, speaker on/off,
                     talk on/off, microphone on/off, playback underruns and drops). volume, talk and mic are
                     saved on the camera; mic off never opens the microphone and removes the audio track from
                     the stream (needs a reboot to apply); beep plays a
                     short tone; off silences the speaker and keeps it off after a reboot; on may need a
                     reboot of the camera to start the speaker. No confirmation is asked.
  picture [show | brightness N | contrast N | saturation N | sharpness N | wb conf|manual | night auto|day|night | reset]
                     the picture, over SSH. N is -50 to 50 (0 = unchanged); the four numbers are saved on the
                     camera and applied at every start. wb and night are not saved (back to the default at a
                     reboot). reset puts the four numbers back to 0. Several words can be combined. Default: show.
  video [show | bitrate N | fps N | gop N]
                     the video encoder settings, over SSH. Default: show. bitrate is the cap in kbit/s (500 to 3000,
                     default 1536); fps is 10, 15, 20 or 25 (default 15; night mode always runs at 10); gop is the key
                     frame interval in seconds (1 to 4, default 2). Saved on the camera, applied at the next restart of the video service: you are
                     asked (default No) whether to restart it now (the stream stops for a few seconds).
                     Resolution and flip/mirror are not available.
  debug [on [MINUTES] | off | status]
                     port 8091 is read-only for the network: 'on' lets the network change settings
                     too (for the PC tuning tool) for MINUTES (default 60, up to 1440) or until
                     'off' or a reboot; over SSH. Default: status
  ntp [status | set SERVER... | off]
                     time sync of the camera (it has no clock of its own and starts in the past), over SSH.
                       set: 1 to 3 servers (host names or IPv4 addresses, for example pool.ntp.org); the camera
                       asks them at start-up and every 6 hours (UDP 123, outgoing only) and saves the choice
                       off: stop and forget the servers (default: off, no traffic at all)
                       status (default): servers, the camera's clock, whether it is believable
  onvif [enable [PORT] | disable | status]
                     ONVIF for NVRs and apps (ONVIF Device Manager, Frigate, Home Assistant): off by default.
                     'enable' (asks first, needs the stream password: rtsp-auth set) opens TCP PORT (default 80)
                     and UDP 3702 on your network; 'disable' closes them. Over SSH. Default: status
  mqtt [status | set | disable | forget | test]
                     optional MQTT client for Home Assistant (a broker on your network): off by default, over SSH.
                       set: asks the broker address, port, user name and password (not shown), then asks (default No)
                         whether to turn it on; the camera connects OUT (no new open port), in plain TCP
                       disable: stop (the settings stay); forget: stop and delete the settings and the password
                       test: one connection attempt with the saved settings; status (default): on or off, the broker
  talk [SECONDS | test]
                     speak to the camera's speaker from this computer's microphone (Ctrl-C stops);
                     'test' sends a 2 second tone. Needs the stream password (rtsp-auth) and
                     ffmpeg or arecord; uses the ONVIF audio backchannel on port 554
  say FILE           play an audio file on the camera's speaker (ffmpeg converts it)
  restore            put the original APP back from your backup
  wipe-sd            erase a card that holds your Wi-Fi password (use it when you are done)
  step NAME          run one step again (doctor, get-kit, prepare-backup-sd,
                     find-camera, backup, image, prepare-flash-sd, flash, verify)
  status             show every step: done / todo / blocked
  cameras            list the cameras you set up (one progress each); switch with --camera NAME
  rename-camera NEW  give the current camera another name (its progress moves with it)
  register           record a camera that ALREADY runs Akam, without redoing backup, image or flash:
                     ./akam --camera NAME --ip ADDRESS [--backup-dir DIR] register
  version            show the version of this program
  self-test          offline checks of the built-in parts (G.711, Digest, state files); changes nothing
  forget-host-key IP forget the remembered SSH host key of a camera (after a reinstall; asks first)
  licenses           show the licences of the software built into this program
  help               this text

Options:
  --dry-run          rehearse: show every command, change nothing, ask nothing
  --kit FILE         use a local kit (tarball with SHA256SUMS next to it, or folder)
  --version X.Y.Z    release to download for get-kit
  --allow-unsigned   accept a kit without a signature (checksum only)
  --expect-sha256 H  require the kit sha256 to start with H (from the release notes)
  --backup-dir DIR   your backup folder (mtdblock0.bin ... mtdblock7.bin)
  --backup-copy DIR  also copy the backup folder to DIR (another medium) and check the copy
  --ip ADDRESS       the camera's IP address (find-camera asks otherwise)
  --identity FILE    your SSH private key (default: ~/.ssh/akam, or --pubkey without .pub)
  --image FILE       image for update (default: the one built by the image step)
  --manual-telnet    never drive the camera's telnet shell: the wizard prints each command and
                     you paste it in your own telnet window (also automatic after 2 failures)
  --out DIR          where the image is written (default ~/akam-image-<name>-<date>)
  --builder native|tools
                     image builder: tools = the bash wizard (Linux, the default there); native = built
                     in this program from your backup (EXPERIMENTAL, not yet validated on hardware)
  --i-understand-untested
                     native builder: build even when the backup is not a validated target
  --comp xz|gzip     override the stock APP compression (not recommended)
  --allow-unknown-stock-comp
                     allow a stock APP compression other than xz/gzip (expert)
  --device DEV       whole microSD disk, for example /dev/sdb
  --pubkey FILE      SSH public key for the flash card (default ~/.ssh/akam.pub)
  --ssid NAME        Wi-Fi name (the password is asked without echo, or set AKAM_WIFI_PASS)
  --profile P        doctor profile: kit | build | all
  --camera NAME      the camera to work on (a short name such as salon: a-z, 0-9, '-', at most 20).
                     Each camera has its own progress and log; a new name starts a new camera. The
                     name is used in the backup and image folders. The first menu run asks for it;
                     progress made before this existed is the camera called "default".
  --state-dir DIR    where progress and the logs are kept
                     (default ${XDG_STATE_HOME:-~/.local/state}/akam)
  --trust-new-host-key FP
                     accept the first SSH host key of a camera when its SHA256 fingerprint is FP
                     (without a terminal; a CHANGED key is always refused)
  --password-file F  read the stream password for talk/say from the first line of F
  --no-color         no colours (NO_COLOR works too)
  --plain            line-based output and prompts, no full-screen menu or widgets
                     (also automatic when not on a terminal; AKAM_PLAIN=1 does the same)

A native binary (experimental) exists: see the releases. From a source checkout, build it with
tools/assistant/build.sh and run it with AKAM_USE_BIN=1 ./akam ... (same arguments, same state files).

Safety: the wizard itself never runs sudo (it prints the command; only the SD tool asks sudo
for the disk work), shows every command before it runs, refuses destructive actions without a
terminal, and never stores your Wi-Fi password. Erasing a card and writing the camera's flash
each need your explicit yes in a red box (one yes/no, default No: Enter, Esc or q mean No).
It never reboots or power-cycles the camera.
